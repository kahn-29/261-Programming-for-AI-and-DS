\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This study examined machine learning and deep learning approaches to predicting real estate asking prices in Vietnam. Data-quality checks reduced 3,500,744 raw listings to 2,397,001 modelling observations. Hierarchical location features, missing-value treatment, text processing, and a chronological holdout provided a common basis for comparing the predictors.

On the held-out test set, LightGBM achieved lower MAE than the embedding-based MLP, consistent with the importance of competitive tabular baselines discussed by \textcite{gorishniy2021revisiting}. Text-enhanced LightGBM reduced test MAE by approximately 5.4\% relative to its structured counterpart. These comparisons describe the evaluated configurations under one chronological split, without isolating an intrinsic advantage of any model architecture.

Among the evaluated configurations, the all-model blend had the lowest observed test MAE (3.659 billion VND), with $R^2=0.794$. Because the blend includes BamiBERT, this result remains provisional pending checkpoint-reload verification. Its observed MAE was approximately 13.4\% below the best evaluated individual predictor and 1.1\% below the smaller structured--BamiBERT blend. The incremental gain should be assessed alongside inference cost once checkpoint correctness has been verified.

These findings apply to advertised asking prices under the study's evaluation protocol, subject to the data-quality and model-selection limitations in Section~\ref{subsec:evaluation-limitations}.

Further work should prioritise checkpoint and text audits, property-level identifiers, and repeated temporal evaluations. Province-, property-type-, and price-range analyses would reveal where errors are concentrated, while transaction-verified targets and uncertainty estimates would support a more realistic assessment of practical valuation performance.
