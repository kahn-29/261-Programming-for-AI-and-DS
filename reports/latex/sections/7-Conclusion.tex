\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This study examined machine learning and deep learning approaches to predicting real estate asking prices in Vietnam. Data-quality checks reduced 3,500,744 raw listings to 2,397,001 modelling observations. Hierarchical location features, missing-value treatment, text processing, and a chronological holdout provided a common basis for comparing the predictors.

Three findings emerge. First, boosted trees provide a strong structured-data baseline: LightGBM achieved lower MAE than the embedding-based MLP, consistent with the importance of competitive tabular baselines discussed by \textcite{gorishniy2021revisiting}. Second, listing descriptions add predictive information: text-enhanced LightGBM improved MAE by approximately 5.4\% over its structured counterpart. Third, combining model families improved the test results further. The all-model blend achieved MAE of 3.659 billion VND and $R^2=0.794$, with MAE approximately 13.4\% below the strongest individual predictor. Its advantage over the smaller structured--BamiBERT blend was only about 1.1\%, suggesting that model complexity should be assessed alongside accuracy.

These findings apply to advertised asking prices under the study's evaluation protocol. The data-quality, text-leakage, checkpoint-verification, and model-selection limits in Section~\ref{subsec:evaluation-limitations} remain material to their interpretation. In particular, the results involving BamiBERT require a verified checkpoint reload before stronger claims can be made.

Further work should prioritise checkpoint and text audits, property-level identifiers, and repeated temporal evaluations. Province-, property-type-, and price-range analyses would reveal where errors are concentrated, while transaction-verified targets and uncertainty estimates would support a more realistic assessment of practical valuation performance.
